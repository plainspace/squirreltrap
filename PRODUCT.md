# Product

register: product

## Product purpose

Squirrel Trap interrupts the moment right before a keyboard-driven app switch
and asks what you are about to do. The answer becomes a to-do. Unfinished
answers stay visible, so the intent you abandoned two switches ago is still in
front of you.

The whole app is one small floating panel that appears on Cmd+Tab and is gone
again in about two seconds. Every design decision follows from that: it is read
in a glance, over the top of whatever you were working on, many times a day.

## Users

One person, on their own Mac, who switches apps constantly and loses the thread
while doing it. Technical, keyboard-driven, already running a real workload on
the screen underneath. Not a team tool, not a project manager, not something
anyone will sit inside for an hour.

## Brand personality

Calm, quiet, factual. The panel is an interruption by design, so it has to earn
the interruption by being fast to read and instantly dismissible. It should feel
like a system surface rather than an app announcing itself.

No cheerleading, no streak-shaming, no "you've got this." One small celebration
when something gets checked off, and nothing else.

## Anti-references

- Habit-tracker gamification: badges, confetti, levels, guilt copy on a broken streak.
- Anything that looks like it wants attention. This thing appears unbidden over
  your work; the second it feels like an ad for itself, it gets uninstalled.
- Dashboard-style density. There are never more than about a dozen items.
- Chrome for chrome's sake: tinted cards, gradients, glass, decorative icons.

## Strategic principles

1. **The panel is glanceable or it is worthless.** It appears mid-task. If it
   takes more than a beat to parse, the user has already Cmd+Tabbed past it.
2. **The resting state is text.** Controls appear when the pointer is near them.
   A list of eight to-dos should look like eight sentences, not eight toolbars.
3. **It follows the system.** Light/dark, accent, contrast and reduce-motion
   settings all come from macOS. The app has no opinion the OS has not already
   expressed.
4. **Dismissal is never blocked.** Escape, click-away, and the close button all
   work instantly, with no confirmation, always.
5. **Support and settings are not the point.** Ko-fi, preferences and snooze
   live in the footer at the lowest possible visual weight. They must be
   findable and must never compete with the input or the list.

## Reference exemplars

Things 3 by Cultured Code is the bar: type-led hierarchy, flat rows, hairline
rules, one reserved accent, and controls that stay out of the way until needed.
Secondary references are Spotlight and Raycast for the appear-over-your-work
panel behaviour.
